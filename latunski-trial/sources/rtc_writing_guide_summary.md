# RTC writing guide — summary of "RTC trial / How to write for the channel"

Source: https://docs.google.com/document/d/141ob7RQ5mJS6jV--wChOvNUidfxB-HsWY5LFX--0fm0/edit (the full doc also contains a complete example script for the Jimmy West / Zaria Hardee case; the trialist cannot access that case's bodycam clips but can watch the finished video at https://youtu.be/Tmakblpxs5k).

## Format rules
- Narration in RED, transcriptions in BLUE.
- Every clip is written as `Play clip <start>-<end>` with a link to the Drive file (they use `&t=<seconds>` on the Drive link) and the transcription under it.
- Editor instructions are written inline as `@editor ...` (sound effects, zooms, images to show, "small fuzz transition").
- Narration breaks: about 45–60 seconds each. Shorter if there is nothing interesting to add; a little longer if there is a lot.
- Up to about 3 minutes of interrogation between narration breaks (not a hard stop, but the realistic maximum).
- Scripts are usually about 700 words, can go up to 1,500.
- Transcriptions are only required for the intro and setup. Full-script transcription is done by another team after the script is approved.

## Content rules
- Everything must be YouTube safe. "Smacked in the head with a hammer, broke his skull" becomes "the victim received blunt force trauma"; "shot in the chest and it destroyed his chest cavity" becomes "received a gunshot wound to the chest". Suspects' swearing is muted by the editor.
- Cut all the fluff: skip rapport-building, keep only the who/what/where/when/why and the moments where the suspect or a witness does something bizarre, emotional or revealing.
- Use a narration break to explain who the person is and where they were, so the interview can start at the juicy part.

## The trial
Write, for the Mark Latunski case:
1. Intro hook & setup (must be highly engaging),
2. First narration point,
3. First clip section.
"If you can find a way to inject a mystery element into this intro hook and setup, you're in with a real shot of being on the team. Mystery and intrigue are our biggest factors in creating a great script."
