## How to write For RTC

Things to do when writing the script:

Narration breaks: should be a maximum of around 45 to 60 seconds. They can be a lot shorter if there isn't anything interesting to include, and they can be a little bit longer if there's heaps of stuff that you can put for the case.
Distance between narration breaks should be up to about three minutes of interrogation. If there's three minutes of interrogation, it doesn't mean it's a hard stop for you to put something in, but that's probably like the realistic maximum for it. If it's really, really, really engaging, you can make it go longer, but that's probably about the point that we're looking to get a narration break in order to add some information that the viewer might not know yet about the case.

All writing must be YouTube safe: If you were going to write something like, "The man was murdered violently with a hammer. He was smacked in the head and it broke his skull," what we would change that to is, "The victim received blunt force trauma." You could also say, "At the editor, show autopsy report, report sketch circling the head."Also, look for things if they are along the lines of "he was shot in the chest and it destroyed his chest cavity." We should say "he received a gunshot wound to the chest."
The wording for this needs to be incredibly YouTube safe. You will see in the transcriptions that the suspect swears a lot in this. All of that will be taken out by the editor and muted.
Note: you will not have to supply written transcriptions for the whole case, only in the intro and set up. Whole script transcriptions are done by another team after the script has been fully written. That being said, transcriptions are extremely important in the intro and setup, as this is what can make or break the case.

Scripts are usually about 700 words, but it can go longer to 1500 words:
Your main goal when writing the case and selecting the clips is to cut out all the fluff. If there's a whole bunch of the start of an interview, a four-hour interview where there's two hours of them building rapport, just delete that. All the viewers want to see is the best juicy bits, so we want to cut anything that seems boring. We just want to know exactly what happened in the story and say if there are parts of the interrogation where the suspect is doing something bizarre or crazy. Same goes for a witness too. If they're really emotional or really upset, those are the things that we want to add in. We want to obviously build a minor part of the story at the start to just get the main explanation, like "Where were you?" or "Why was this happening?" We need the who, what, where, when, and why, but we don't want to go through the massive rapport-building stage. If possible, we could use a narration break to explain who the person was in the case first and where they were, so that we can get into the juicier part of the interview to keep the pace high.
