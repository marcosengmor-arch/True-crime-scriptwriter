# How Two Detectives Got Three Hours Out of Mark Latunski — Interrogation Breakdown

An analysis of the December 28 and December 30, 2019 Michigan State Police interviews of Mark Latunski, written for interrogation-format scripting. Timestamps refer to the released footage (PT 1 = Dec 28 camera 1; PT 4 and PT 3 = Dec 30, first and second half).

## The starting position

The detectives walked in with more than most interviewers ever get: a body found by consent search, a suspect who had asked to speak to a detective, and chat logs between the suspect and the victim that D/Sgt Dirkse had already read. The risk was not getting a confession. The risk was getting a confession that was unusable, from a man who, within the first minute, said his name was a stolen identity. Everything they did over the next three hours was about keeping him talking while making the record hold up.

## Phase 1 — Let the name go (0:00–0:08)

Latunski opens by denying that he is Mark Latunski. D/Sgt Moore does not argue. He asks what name Latunski prefers, how to spell it, and lets him sign the Miranda form as "Wilk Olykos Vilks" with date of birth "unknown" (PT 1, 0:07:11–0:08:03). Arguing about identity would have cost the interview before it started. Writing the signature down as given, with the time on it, kept the waiver clean and gave the court a man who understood every question he was asked.

## Phase 2 — Fifteen minutes of nothing (0:08–0:20)

Trust funds, New York bankers, Flint Ink, Norwegian coming-of-age, the Thomas of Wales clan. Moore asks only short, factual follow-ups ("what did you do for Flint Ink?", "how long did you work there?"). This is the rapport phase, and it also quietly builds the biography the prosecutor will need: twelve years as a quality manager, a job lost in early 2019, paid companionship since April. He is calm, articulate and oriented. That matters later.

## Phase 3 — The pivot question (0:20:24)

Moore ties the ramble back to the reason they are there in one sentence: "you let the troopers go in and search your house and they see a man… who is that man?" Latunski answers with Grindr, then with the word "agreement". From here the detectives mostly ask "so what was the agreement?" and "what did you do?" and let him fill the silence. The full description of the killing comes without a single confrontation (0:36–0:40). The knife's location follows immediately (0:39:37) and is confirmed by the search team the same day.

## Phase 4 — Lock the physical record (0:47–1:00)

Once the narrative is on tape, they go back over it for the details that evidence can check: where in the house ("it was all downstairs"), position on the floor, the sketch of the room (0:55:13), whether anyone helped ("I did it all by myself", 0:57:19), which night ("the 24th into the 25th", 0:59:36). None of this is for the viewer. All of it is for the case file. For a script, this section is where the clips get short and the narration carries the weight.

## Phase 5 — Agree with him (1:00–1:30)

Dirkse: "that shows me the type of person you are" (1:00:13), "you're a man of your word" (1:00:20), "I want to commend you because not everybody could do this" (1:26:50), "we're open to a lot of things" (2:42:10). Moore on the pack dynamics: "I play that with my dog. I'm the alpha dog in this house" (1:23:18). This is minimization and affirmation. It looks grotesque in isolation and it is the reason the text message ("I killed Kevin Bacon, Kevin Bacon is my hero", 1:12:48), the Christmas dinner (1:13:39) and the admission that he did not wash (1:08:36) all come out unprompted. The viewer needs the narration to say what the detectives are doing, or the detectives look complicit.

## Phase 6 — Other victims, three ways (1:36–2:34)

- Soft, from Dirkse: "has this ever happened before?" (1:36:54). No.
- Broader, from Moore: "have you ever been requested in the past… have you ever assisted?" (1:47:08). "There have been people who have hinted at it."
- Direct, with the demeanor read: "with your demeanor, you're so calm… is this really the only person?" (1:54:01). "You're completely wrong. However…" and the story about a field, a shovel and the cemetery plot of Mark Latunski.
- Leverage: "I'm going to have a cadaver dog go out" (1:57:26). He volunteers the dog, the horse and the bucket of sand on the hyacinths (1:58:19), then: "if it doesn't, your cadaver dog is broken."
- Evidence: the November runaway (2:06:08), framed as "if that was consensual, what caused him to flee?" He answers with the kilt.
- Final, moral: "is that the truth? You've been truthful so far" (2:33:11). "I can't tell a lie for anything."

Six asks, each with a different footing. None produced a second victim, and the K9 sweep came back nil. But the sequence is the spine of the episode, because it is the only place where the detectives and the viewer want the same thing.

## Phase 7 — Consent, samples, photographs (2:59–3:16)

After the break, Dirkse reads the consent form line by line and has Latunski initial each part and write the date and time himself (3:05:23). Fingernail swabs are taken on camera while Latunski explains that he deliberately did not wash (3:10:39). The photographs show no injuries, which closes off any later self-defense claim. Then Moore tells him plainly that he is going to jail tonight (3:16:01). No bargaining, no promise.

## Phase 8 — The second interview (Dec 30)

Two days later, after arraignment and after his lawyers told him not to talk, a detective re-Mirandizes him in the jail interview room (PT 4, 0:03:11–0:06:25) and gets a yes. The questions are narrow: the 2013 "stroke of a pen" call, the November runaway, the man from New York in October, and once more, "no other victims?" (PT 3, 0:24:16). Each answer is on tape, with counsel's advice acknowledged by the suspect on camera (PT 4, 0:00:36). This is the cleanest piece of the whole file.

## What this means for the script

- The interview has a natural three-act shape: name (0:00–0:20), confession (0:20–1:00), other victims (1:36–2:34). The Dec 30 interview is the epilogue.
- The graphic middle (0:36–1:06, 2:20–2:42) must be carried by narration. The detectives' questions in those stretches are also unusable because they repeat the detail.
- The affirmation tactics need one narration line of context each time they appear, or the audience turns on the detectives.
- The "is this the only one?" sequence should be cut as a ladder, in order, with the cadaver dog as the rung the audience remembers.
